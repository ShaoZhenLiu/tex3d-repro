Proposition 2b: unique normalized invariant and a limitation of identification

Order the four corners (00,10,01,11). A linear contrast c00*A00+c10*A10+c01*A01+c11*A11 with c11=1 is invariant to every additive nuisance b+e1(i)+e2(j) if and only if (c00,c10,c01,c11)=(1,-1,-1,1).

Proof: annihilating the main-view indicator gives c10+c11=0; annihilating the wrist indicator gives c01+c11=0; annihilating constants gives c00+c10+c01+c11=0. These force the unique contrast. The nuisance space has dimension three in the four-dimensional corner space. The one remaining quotient coordinate is the interaction. This is a standard factorial-contrast fact applied to a specified rendering-error model, not a new general mathematical identity.

Native total joint displacement is NOT identifiable from the four synthesis outputs under unrestricted additive error, even with the native clean corner n00 known. Set b=AR(0,0)-n00. For any action vector q, the possible native map AN_q(i,j)=AR(i,j)-b-i*q has that same known native clean corner and the same interaction, while changing its native joint displacement by -q. Additional calibration or structural assumptions are necessary to infer the joint displacement. The interaction estimand becomes useful only if it can be made behaviorally consequential under unilateral protection, as Proposition 1 quantifies.

An empirical rejection is informative: if measured IR-IN is not smaller than the ordinary matched joint-displacement error, then the assumed additive renderer-error family is not supported at those states/textures. The identity remains true; the proposed method's justification fails.
